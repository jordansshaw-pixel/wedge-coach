# Objection Bank — First-3-Minute Pushback from Healthcare CEOs/COOs

The fifteen objections healthcare leaders most commonly raise in the first three minutes of an unsolicited or warm-intro vCIO/vCAIO pitch — what they actually mean underneath, what NOT to say, and a non-defensive direction for what to say.

Wedge Coach uses this bank to (1) generate realistic in-character pushback during DRILL mode, and (2) inform diagnoses when a user's pitch failed to anticipate or absorb a foreseeable objection.

---

## 1. "We already have an MSP."

**What they actually mean:** *"Switching is expensive and risky. I will not switch unless something is meaningfully broken — and I'm not going to admit to a stranger that something is broken."*

**Don't:** Argue the merits of switching.
**Do:** Reframe. *"I'm not asking you to switch. I'm asking 30 minutes to show you what we'd find in a Visibility Audit of your environment — you keep the report whether we work together or not."*

---

## 2. "We just passed our HIPAA audit."

**What they actually mean:** *"Why are you trying to scare me? Or — alternatively — what do you know that I don't?"*

**Don't:** Imply the audit was inadequate.
**Do:** Honor the win and pivot. *"That's a good outcome. The pattern we see in ortho groups in the 90-day window after a passed audit is [specific finding]. Worth confirming you're clean on that one specifically."*

---

## 3. "How much per provider per month?"

**What they actually mean:** *"I need to know if this is in the no-go price band before I spend more time."*

**Don't:** Quote a number on the spot or deflect entirely.
**Do:** Give a band tied to scope. *"For orthopedic groups in your seat range, the comprehensive vCIO/vCAIO engagement runs in a band of $X–$Y per provider per month, and the Visibility Audit that determines exact scope is fixed-fee at $Z. The audit alone tells you whether the engagement is justified."*

---

## 4. "Our IT is fine."

**What they actually mean:** *"I'm not going to admit a problem to a stranger on a cold call. Or — I genuinely don't know what's broken and I'm tired of being told."*

**Don't:** Insist they have problems.
**Do:** Make it specific and easy to disagree with. *"Three things are very likely true in your environment based on what's typical for ortho groups your size. If even one of them is wrong in your case, I want to hear about it — because that means you're doing something most groups aren't."*

---

## 5. "I have a hard stop in 5 minutes."

**What they actually mean:** *"Earn more time, fast — or don't, and we both move on."*

**Don't:** Say "this will only take a minute" and then pitch for 5.
**Do:** Compress to a single specific. *"Then I'll use 60 seconds. Three orthopedic groups in your size range got OCR letters in the last 90 days — same gap each time. If that gap is in your environment, you'd want to know now, not after the letter arrives. Worth 20 minutes next week to find out?"*

---

## 6. "What makes you different from [competitor]?"

**What they actually mean:** *"Tell me your wedge in one sentence. If you fumble this, this conversation is done."*

**Don't:** List five differentiators or denigrate the competitor.
**Do:** One sentence, specific, falsifiable. *"They're a generalist MSP. We work only with orthopedic surgical groups, and our CEO is CISSP-ISSAP credentialed in security architecture. That combination doesn't exist at most firms in our size range."*

---

## 7. "We're too small for what you offer."

**What they actually mean:** *"I think you'll oversell me — or I think you'll deprioritize me."*

**Don't:** Say "we work with practices of all sizes."
**Do:** Confirm the fit. *"We don't take ortho clients under 50 seats. You're in the size band where our Visibility Audit and vCIO model are designed to work. If you're concerned about being deprioritized, our clients between 50–150 seats get the same quarterly leadership cadence as our 400-seat clients."*

---

## 8. "We're moving to the EHR vendor's hosted version."

**What they actually mean:** *"I think IT is becoming a solved problem."*

**Don't:** Argue against hosted EHR.
**Do:** Surface the gaps a hosted EHR doesn't solve. *"Hosted EHR solves the EHR uptime problem. It doesn't solve endpoint security, BAAs with your other vendors, OCR audit response, or the asset inventory you'll need for SOC 2. That's the layer we work on."*

---

## 9. "AI is hype."

**What they actually mean:** *"I've been pitched four AI-flavored things this month and don't trust any of them."*

**Don't:** Defend AI broadly.
**Do:** Agree where they're right, then narrow. *"You're right that most of the AI pitch in healthcare is overstated. Two specific places it's not hype in an ortho practice today are [specific use case 1] and [specific use case 2]. Our vCAIO role exists to help you ignore the first 90% and focus on those two."*

---

## 10. "Why should I trust your security claims?"

**What they actually mean:** *"Prove you have receipts, or this conversation is over."*

**Don't:** Promise more than you can document.
**Do:** Hand them an artifact. *"Three pieces of documentation: our SIEM platform's deployment list (which includes [class of institution]), our CEO's CISSP-ISSAP credential verification, and an anonymized case study of an ortho group whose insurance premium dropped [%] at renewal after we onboarded. I can send all three before our next call."*

---

## 11. "Send me a deck."

**What they actually mean:** *"I want to get off this call without saying no."*

**Don't:** Send a 30-page generic deck.
**Do:** Trade the deck for a small commitment. *"I'd rather not send a generic deck — they're useless. What I will send is a one-page snapshot of what a Visibility Audit produces for an ortho group your size. If after reading it you don't want to talk again, just say so. Fair?"*

---

## 12. "Our cyber insurance is fine."

**What they actually mean:** *"We haven't had a breach yet."*

**Don't:** Predict doom.
**Do:** Reframe to renewal economics. *"The bar that matters isn't whether your insurance is fine today — it's where your premium goes at renewal. Carriers in the ortho space are repricing aggressively. Two ortho groups we work with had carriers extend coverage at favorable terms because we documented their controls. The question is whether your renewal looks like that or the other direction."*

---

## 13. "We don't have budget right now."

**What they actually mean:** *"I don't see ROI yet."*

**Don't:** Try to find money in their budget.
**Do:** Decouple. *"Understood. The Visibility Audit is a fixed-fee engagement and ortho groups in your size range typically fund it from their existing IT/compliance line, not new budget. The question for the audit isn't budget — it's whether the findings would justify a conversation about what to do next."*

---

## 14. "Our IT director handles this."

**What they actually mean:** *"Talk to them — but I'm the one who decides."*

**Don't:** Brush off the IT director.
**Do:** Honor the relationship and earn the executive layer. *"I'd want to bring your IT director into any technical conversation. But the vCIO/vCAIO layer is specifically what sits at the executive table — it's the layer that translates IT decisions into board language. Your IT director would be an essential collaborator, not the decision point."*

---

## 15. "I'll think about it."

**What they actually mean:** *"No."*

**Don't:** Accept it as a maybe.
**Do:** Make it a specific yes-or-no. *"Fair. Two things — would you be open to a fixed-fee Visibility Audit (no further commitment) in the next 30 days? If the answer is no, I'd rather know now so I'm not pestering you. If the answer is 'maybe later,' tell me what would have to change for it to be a yes."*

---

## How Wedge Coach uses this bank

- During DRILL mode: select 1–3 objections that fit the persona's recent context and mood, deploy them naturally during the user's pitch.
- During DIAGNOSE mode: when the user failed to absorb a foreseeable objection (one that the persona's context made obvious), name it as the inflection moment.
- The diagnosis never punishes the user for not memorizing the bank — it points to *why* a specific objection should have been anticipated from the persona's recent context.
