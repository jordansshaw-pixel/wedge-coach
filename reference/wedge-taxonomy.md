# Wedge Taxonomy — The Six Archetypes

A *wedge* is the one sentence that earns 30 more seconds of attention from a healthcare CEO/COO who has been pitched five MSPs this quarter.

There are six archetypes that work in the orthopedic surgical 50–500 seat range. Each works on certain buyer types and fails on others. Most credible MSP sellers end up combining two.

Wedge Coach uses this taxonomy to (1) recognize when a pitch is leaning on a wedge versus generic claims, and (2) name which wedge is emerging from the user's reps in the Wedge Synthesis output.

---

## 1. Vertical Specialist Wedge

**The claim:** "We work *only* with orthopedic surgical groups."

**Why it works:** Healthcare CEOs are tired of generalists. A specialist signals depth, vocabulary fluency, and existing playbooks. It earns the right to talk about clinical-workflow data structures.

**Failure mode:** The claim is easy to make and hard to back. If the seller cannot immediately produce numbers (groups served, EHRs hosted, ortho-specific SOPs), the wedge collapses.

**Credible form:** *"We work only with orthopedic groups in the 50–500 seat range. We host EHR for [N] of them. Our support SOPs are written for orthopedic clinical workflows specifically — not healthcare in general."*

**Works best on:** Founder Surgeon-CEOs, ASC Operators, Aging Founders. Anyone who has been frustrated by generalist MSPs.

**Fails on:** PE-backed CEOs may worry "boutique = small," need to be reassured on scale.

---

## 2. Outcome-Named Wedge

**The claim:** A specific, named, numbered outcome from a specific, named (or anonymized) client.

**Why it works:** Healthcare CEOs run on metrics. A specific outcome is falsifiable, repeatable, and translatable to a board.

**Failure mode:** Outcomes that are too generic ("we save you money," "we reduce risk") sound identical to every other MSP pitch. Numbers without context are equally weak ("we improved security 40%" — measured how?).

**Credible form:** *"We dropped a 240-seat orthopedic group's cyber insurance premium by 38% in 6 months — measured against their renewal quote, documented by the carrier."*

**Works best on:** PE-backed CEOs, Skeptical CFOs, Burned-Before COOs. Anyone whose attention is held by numbers.

**Fails on:** Founder Surgeon-CEOs and Aging Founders who don't run on metrics — they trust people, not numbers.

---

## 3. Methodology-Named Wedge

**The claim:** Your firm runs a specific, named methodology that has a name they can repeat to their CFO.

**Why it works:** A named methodology signals that what you do is repeatable, documented, and transferable. It also gives the buyer a phrase they can bring to their next meeting ("I want them to do their 30-Day Visibility Audit before we sign anything").

**Failure mode:** A named methodology with no actual structure behind it ("our 5-Pillar Excellence Model") triggers immediate eye-rolls. The name has to be tied to a real artifact: an audit checklist, an asset model, a deliverable.

**Credible form:** *"Before we ever quote a managed services contract, we run our 30-Day Visibility Audit — three weeks of asset discovery and one week of finding-prioritization — and you keep the report whether you sign with us or not."*

**Works best on:** Compliance-Obsessed CEOs, Skeptical CFOs, Audit-Fatigued COOs. Anyone who wants structure they can verify.

**Fails on:** Founder Surgeon-CEOs who hear "methodology" as "consultant-speak."

---

## 4. AI-First (vCAIO) Wedge

**The claim:** "Most MSPs will sell you a help desk. We embed a fractional Chief AI Officer."

**Why it works:** Healthcare CEOs are being asked about AI by their boards, surgeons, and competitors. Most MSPs respond with "we use AI" (which means nothing). A vCAIO offering — a fractional executive guiding AI adoption — is a different offer entirely.

**Failure mode:** "We use AI in our security tools" or "our MSP includes AI" is generic. AI-as-feature is not a wedge. AI-as-strategic-role is.

**Credible form:** *"Our vCAIO sits with your leadership quarterly to map where AI actually moves the needle in an orthopedic group — ambient scribes, RCM automation, scheduling optimization — and where it doesn't yet. You get a 12-month roadmap. Most MSPs can't do this because they've never sat in those meetings."*

**Works best on:** AI-Curious CEOs, PE-Backed CEOs, Multi-State Group leaders. Anyone whose board is asking "what's our AI strategy?"

**Fails on:** Burned-Before COOs and Compliance-Obsessed CEOs who view AI as another risk surface, not an opportunity.

---

## 5. Founder-Credibility Wedge

**The claim:** Specific credentialed authority of the firm's principal that is unusual in the MSP market.

**Why it works:** A CISSP-ISSAP credential, a published security framework, a former-CISO background, or a vertical-specific clinical credential signals that the firm is led by someone who earned the right to talk about risk — not by a salesperson with a title.

**Failure mode:** Vague "decades of experience" or "industry leader" framing reads as bio-puffery. The credential or background has to be specific, verifiable, and meaningful to the buyer.

**Credible form:** *"Our CEO is CISSP-ISSAP certified — which means he's a security architect, not a business owner who hires security people. He personally reviews every orthopedic client's environment for OCR-reportable gaps before we onboard."*

**Works best on:** Audit-Fatigued COOs, Burned-Before COOs, Compliance-Obsessed CEOs. Anyone who needs to trust a person, not a brand.

**Fails on:** PE-Backed CEOs who care about the *system*, not the founder, and worry about key-person risk.

---

## 6. Contrarian-Refusal Wedge

**The claim:** What you *refuse* to do that other MSPs accept.

**Why it works:** Refusal signals discipline. "We don't take ortho clients under 50 seats and we don't do break-fix" tells the buyer exactly what kind of firm you are — and gives them a reason to believe you'll be excellent at the work you do take.

**Failure mode:** Refusal that sounds arrogant ("we only work with serious clients") backfires. Refusal that sounds disciplined ("we don't take clients we can't measurably improve in 90 days") works.

**Credible form:** *"We don't onboard a practice we haven't run a Visibility Audit on first. If the audit shows we can't measurably reduce your insurance premium and your downtime within 6 months, we tell you so before you sign. Most MSPs will take any check that clears."*

**Works best on:** Skeptical CFOs, PE-Backed CEOs, Burned-Before COOs. Anyone who has been oversold before.

**Fails on:** AI-Curious CEOs in growth mode who hear "refuse" as "rigid."

---

## How to use this taxonomy in diagnosis

When diagnosing a drill, identify:

1. **Did the user lean on any of the six archetypes?** If no — the pitch was generic, and the diagnosis should say so directly.
2. **Did the leaned-on archetype match the persona?** A Vertical Specialist wedge on a PE-Backed CEO needs reinforcement on scale. A Founder Credibility wedge on a PE-Backed CEO has a key-person-risk problem to address.
3. **Was the wedge backed with a specific?** A wedge claim with no specific is fluff. "We're orthopedic specialists" is not a Vertical Specialist Wedge — it's a label without substance. The Vertical Specialist Wedge requires numbers ("N groups," "N EHR instances hosted," "N orthopedic-specific SOPs").
4. **Across 5 reps, which archetype is *emerging*?** That is the Wedge Synthesis question. The answer is rarely "one of the six." It's usually "two of these, combined, work for the user."
